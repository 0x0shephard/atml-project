# Project ledger

The single place for **what we know, what ran, who owns what, and what we decided**. Update it in
the same commit as the results it describes. Rules are at the bottom.

- **Goal:** a NeurIPS-level paper on why compressed reasoning models fail. The working claim is
  that they mostly fail because they **can't stop**, not because they talk themselves out of a
  right answer, and that this holds across quantization, pruning, low rank and KV-cache
  compression.
- **Paper we answer:** Lotfi, Kirichenko, Li, Liu, *Quantized Reasoning Models Think They Need to
  Think Longer, but They Do Not*, arXiv [2606.00206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00206), NeurIPS
  2026 oral. They report that the share of failures where the model "had the right answer, then
  lost it" rises from 26% to 52% under AWQ W3, and they fix it with a penalty on words like
  "wait" and "but" (5 models, 1.5B–32B, 3 quantization methods).
- **Target venue:** NeurIPS 2027 (the deadline is usually mid-May; check the call). ICML 2027
  (late January) is possible only if the scale-up in W2 finishes by early January.
- **Team plan and briefs for each member:** [docs/TEAM_PLAN.md](docs/TEAM_PLAN.md).

## Headline findings so far

Each finding cites the run folders it comes from.

1. **Wrong answers are mostly cut-off chains.**
   - R1 full size: 24% of all chains hit the 8k limit; 3-bit: 41%.
   - Classic "wrote the right answer, then a different final answer" cases are rare: 4–12 per
     run. (`results/r1-1.5b/*/none`)
2. **Forcing an answer recovers much of the loss, so the model knows the answer but can't stop.**
   - Low-rank (asvd-r95): 33% → 49% at 4k on the 120 calibration problems, close to base.
     Forced answers for the other 240 problems are still missing.
   - 3-bit: 44.4% → 49.4%.
   - 50% pruned: 53.1% → 56.7%.
   - Qwen3 4-bit: 52.5% → 67.5%. (`scored_budget_*.jsonl`)
3. **Lotfi's rise is not reproduced with plain rounding (RTN).**
   - R1 full size had-it share: 26%, matching their 26%. At 32k it is 26–30%.
   - RTN W3: 18% at 8k and 24% at 32k, against their 52%.
   - It does rise for pruning and low rank: wanda-50 is 38% at 32k, asvd-r95 38% at 8k.
   - The AWQ test (W3) decides whether the method explains the gap.
4. **Longer limits help but don't cure it.**
   - At 32k, R1 3-bit reaches 55%, yet 22.5% are still cut off and many loop verbatim.
   - At 32k, 4-bit reaches 80%, close to base's 82.5%. (`results-32k-ext/`)
5. **Qwen3-1.7B is far more fragile than R1-1.5B.**
   - 4-bit: −15 points, against R1's −2.5.
   - 3-bit: broken, 0.8% right.
   - 50% pruned: −41 points.
   - The RTN code has no bug. Qwen3's activation outliers are 5–10× larger, so the same weight
     error moves it about 5× further (KL 0.13 against 0.03 at W4).
6. **The method checks hold up.**
   - The answer detector is about 80% precise after its fix; two blind labellers agreed 42/42.
   - Continuing cut-off chains to 32k matches fresh 32k runs.
   - KV-int4 is within 1 point of base.
   - AWQ halves R1's 3-bit drift from full size compared with RTN (KL 0.113 against 0.226).

## Rules

1. **Own your folders.** Each task row names its output folder. Two people (or two Kaggle
   sessions) must never write the same run folder. If you need one that's taken, ask its owner.
2. **Log every session.** Add a Run log line when you pull a session's results, even a failed
   or cancelled one, and say why.
3. **Commit results with their ledger change.** Pull (`kaggle/pull.sh`), score
   (`python -m crs.run score --runs 'results/<model>/<setting>/*'`), rerun
   `scripts/ledger_table.py --vs-base`, and paste the new snapshot. Update the task row,
   then commit everything together. Run `git pull --rebase` before pushing.
4. **Write decisions down.** Anything that changes a setting, a threshold or the paper's claim
   goes in Decisions with its reason.
5. **Never commit secrets.** No Kaggle or Hugging Face tokens: `~/.kaggle` stays out of the repo.
   The repo is public.
